## Evidence-Based Professional Positioning



Professional claims should describe demonstrated capability without exceeding the strength of available evidence.



Claims should answer:



\- What security activity was performed?

\- What type of system or attack scenario was assessed?

\- What security capability was demonstrated?

\- What evidence supports the claim?

\- What security or business relevance resulted?

## Interview Evidence Framework



Interview answers should follow:



SITUATION



↓



SECURITY OBJECTIVE



↓



TESTING APPROACH



↓



EVIDENCE



↓



FINDING



↓



SECURITY IMPACT



↓



REMEDIATION / LESSON



Example:



Situation:

An enterprise-style GenAI system was assessed for prompt-injection and jailbreak risk.



Security Objective:

Determine whether adversarial instructions could influence protected model behavior.



Testing Approach:

Structured adversarial prompt scenarios were evaluated and documented.



Evidence:

DAY30-EV-014.



Finding:

The assessment demonstrated how prompt-level attacks can challenge instruction integrity and security-control assumptions.



Security Impact:

Successful manipulation could affect confidentiality, integrity, authorization, or downstream system behavior.



Remediation:

Apply instruction trust separation, input/output controls, policy enforcement, monitoring, and adversarial retesting.



## Professional Claim Rules



Professional statements must:



1\. Be supported by evidence.

2\. Avoid claiming production access where none existed.

3\. Avoid implying unauthorized exploitation.

4\. Distinguish laboratory assessment from real-world client engagements.

5\. Describe methodology accurately.

6\. Explain security relevance.

7\. Preserve evidence traceability.



## Lab 04 Success Criteria
